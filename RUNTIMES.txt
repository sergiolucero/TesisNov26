OBSERVATIONS
------------
* The whole unit suite is sub-second of actual test time; ~1.5 s of the 2.08 s
  is import start-up. All 32 tests pass.
* The quick SAA path costs ~13-14 s and is dominated by the 40 train / 80 test
  scenario draws plus pattern search, not by the PDE solves.
* The full-benchmark forward solve is cheap (3.2 s in-run: 9 wells, 80x40,
  100 transport steps). Scale tests on the PDE kernel are therefore cheap;
  the cost lives in the optimization loop.
* run_optimization.py --stage opt on the full benchmark did NOT finish within
  1800 s on 1 vCPU (rc=124, killed; only figures/well_layout.png written,
  identical q* => identical result to the quick run is not yet available).
  Treat the full opt stage as a long run (overnight class) or reduce
  saa.M_train/M_test, optim.n_starts or optim.max_evals in the YAML.
* `run_optimization.py` now logs stage timing and per-iteration optimizer
  progress ([opt] lines), so a long run no longer looks silent; `fleet_status.sh`
  shows the same job sentinels and the last log line.
* Note the difference in well counts: --quick shrinks wells.n (4 wells,
  total 60 m3/d at --rate 0.3) versus the full config (9 wells, 135 m3/d).

FLOAT32 REPAIR + HOLDOUT GUARD (2026-11-18)
-------------------------------------------
Root cause of the 160x96 float32 transport NaN: the BiCGSTAB *scalars*
(<rhat,r>, ||r||, beta/omega) were accumulated in float32; the reduction error
sits exactly at the 1e-5 tolerance and decides convergence. Fix: promote only
those reductions to float64 (vectors and stencil matvec stay float32), switch
SCALAR_FP64 / TESIS_SCALAR_FP64=0 for A/B.

A/B on one T4, 160x96, M=40, S=19, nsteps=100, float32, 4 reps per setting:
  TESIS_SCALAR_FP64=0 : res=nan, converged=False, stalled, 18/19 finite -- 4/4
  TESIS_SCALAR_FP64=1 : res=9.99e-06, converged, 33 it/solve, 19/19 finite -- 4/4
Cost per candidate*scenario on a T4 at 160x96: float64 0.381 s, float32 0.138 s
(2.8x). End-to-end float32 opt run (M=40): 5 sweeps, all finite, gates pass,
results written in 527.9 s (= 105 s per 19-candidate sweep).

Also fixed: plot_distributions crashed on float32 objective values (np.histogram
refuses 20 bins over the tiny float32 range) AFTER a successful optimization and
BEFORE metrics.json was written, so a whole GPU run produced no numbers. Cast to
float64 in the plot helper; regression test tests/test_report_float32.py.

Scope limit: the promotion fixes M=40 but NOT M=100 -- a float32 run at M=100
(batch 1900) still lost 1/19 candidates to a NaN residual after 28 iterations and
was aborted in the first sweep by _finite_or_raise (8 min of VM instead of 45).
Rule: float32 for M<=40, float64 for M>=100. Fixing M=100 would need the whole
BiCGSTAB recurrence in float64 (float32 stencil as the operator only).

Holdout guard (new): saa.M_holdout > 0 builds a third, disjoint archive and
optim.holdout_every sweeps evaluates the incumbent on it during the search,
logging rel/cvarG/J, recording them in metrics.stages.opt.holdout and adding a
holdout_reliability gate. Cost at M_holdout=40: 6 s on a T4, 2 s on an A100.
It is what catches "the training tail made the policy look compliant".

Verifier note: SCRIPTS/check_tail.py now rebuilds the run through
run_optimization's own constructors. The first version re-derived the grid by
hand as Grid(ny, nx) -- physics.fv2d.Grid is (nx, ny) -- so it verified a
transposed grid with a different KL basis (n_kl 1392 vs 1529) and made a correct
run look unreproducible.

THE 3D WELLS WERE IN THE WRONG CELLS (found 2026-11-23)
-------------------------------------------------------
Found by verifying physics/fv3d.py against analytics, not by looking at results.
Grid3D.centres() returned np.meshgrid(x, y, z, indexing="ij"), i.e. (nx, ny, nz),
while fields are (nz, ny, nx) -- Grid3D.index is k*nx*ny + j*nx + i and
FlowModel3D._build_matrix reshapes that way. gaussian_well_weights_3d builds the
kernel from centres() and ends with (w/w.sum()).ravel(), so the weight for cell
(i,j,k) landed at flat i*ny*nz + j*nz + k instead of (k*ny+j)*nx + i. On a cubic
test grid a well requested at (3.5, 2.5, 2.0) landed at (0.5, 2.5, 3.5).

Nothing in a run could show it: metrics.json reports the REQUESTED coordinates,
and the z-screen mask was applied to a coordinate array that was itself
transposed. Fix: centres() returns (zz, yy, xx) = meshgrid(z, y, x, "ij") and
returns xx, yy, zz. Regression tests: test_well_kernel_lands_on_the_requested_cell,
test_centres_are_in_field_order (tests/test_fv3d_torch_colab.py).

VERIFICATION UPGRADE (tests/test_fv3d_reference.py, 18 tests, 1.8 s)
  * pulse moments: D_long = alpha_l v + v dx/2 to 10%, D_transverse = alpha_t v on
    both axes (read before the no-flow walls bound the variance), centroid at the
    pore velocity K grad / phi -- a direct measurement of the dispersion tensor.
  * flux-inlet front vs the analytic swept front 0.5 C0 erfc((x-vt)/2 sqrt(Dt)):
    rel-L2 1.1e-2 at dx=1 m, 4.0e-3 at dx=0.5 m (3.4e-3 with v dx/2 added to D),
    first order in dx, inside the G3.1 gate of 1.5e-2. The classical Ogata-Banks
    formula is the Dirichlet solution and does NOT fit a mass-flux inlet: it
    differs by a shift of D/v (5-10 cells) that does not shrink with dx. The
    earlier "0.41 shape error" was the wrong reference.
  * geometry guards: Grid3D.centres() axis order (production and physics/fv3d),
    transport_run3d accepting a SET of source cells (plane inlets), mask/screen
    behaviour.
